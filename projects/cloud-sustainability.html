Using AI to Make Cloud Computing More Sustainable

78% of business leaders say they've adopted cloud technology — but more than half can't see the return on it. I built a weighted scoring system plus an LLM call that produces sustainability-first cloud deployment plans for startups who can't afford a consultant.

The gap this fills

Cloud has real potential to cut environmental impact, but small organisations rarely have a specialist who can make sustainability-literate deployment decisions. I checked the two existing AI cloud advisory tools: neither treats sustainability as a pillar, and one is locked to a single provider with under 5% market share. So I built one.

How the decision matrix works

Six inputs — budget, storage, expertise, optional security and growth needs, and a 1–100 sustainability weighting — are scored for AWS, Google Cloud and Azure on cost, scalability, sustainability (PUE and WUE), coverage and security. Whichever priority the business names gets its weighting doubled. I first planned this on Vertex AI Agent Builder, but the logic was more code than agent: a Python script makes the calculated decision and a single Gemini call explains it, which cuts the risk of hallucination.

Tested against real companies

A synthetic startup ($200/month, 3TB, scalability first) returned a 4,000-word plan. To stress-test it properly I fed in the early-stage profiles of three real companies and compared the advice to the route they actually took.

Full test cases are in the results/ folder of the repo.

The honest limitation

Across every test the agent leans toward Google Cloud. Because it calls Gemini — itself a Google product — that is a real risk of bias toward its own ecosystem. A future version should combine several models from different providers rather than trusting one LLM's judgement. On ethics, the tool only processes business parameters and stores nothing after the session, so it stays clear of personal data while being designed with GDPR Article 22 in mind.
